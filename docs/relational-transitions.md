## Authority and applicability

Owner-authorized adoption: 2026-10-07. Baseline: commit `8acccfc31df02038335e525266a58f1843f03265`, profile blob `9e3dfc98db7965517419393b38ee650878d6b813`. The authoritative profile is `relational-systems` version `v1.2.0`, with 94 rules: the unchanged inherited 87 and seven appended `RSR-DYN-001`–`RSR-DYN-007` rules at revision `r1`.

These controls track changes in evidence, belief, permission, capacity, recurrence, reception, and resolution. They are genre-neutral: applicability follows a consequential semantic opportunity, not a genre label, author, emotional palette, required motif, or mandated ending. They can support analysis, explanation, ordinary interactions, institutional accounts, and authored scenes. A technique being available never makes it obligatory in every passage.

Runtime applicability uses only the existing experiential and interpretive layers. All seven atoms are advisory, register-priority, and use the existing `manual-review` validator. Applicable unassessed findings remain **unknown**. The engine does not detect semantic opportunities inside free text; the narrower triggers below guide editorial review. An opportunity that is genuinely absent is editorially **not applicable**, with a reason; missing assessment is unknown. Editorial records are not newly supported request fields or passing engine findings.

The fixed order remains safety → semantic fidelity → accessibility equivalence → operative clarity → domain correctness → register fit → ornament. Operative instructions, permissions, consequences, warnings, and recovery must remain explicit where required. Interpretive openness cannot remove essential meaning from an accessibility equivalent. No automatic semantic judgment, quality score, measured reader effect, or new generation capability is introduced.

## Atomic acceptance register

Review owner: an identified editor or analyst for each actual assessment. Status: **atomized; advisory/manual review; semantic and audience success unassessed**. The following synthetic positive, negative, and boundary probes define acceptance questions; they are not executed reader-effect tests.

| Atom and semantic opportunity | Positive probe | Negative probe | Boundary probe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `RSR-DYN-001`: evidence access or inference changes meaning or action | `DYN-001-P`: an analyst sees residue; an earlier record supports the reader's narrower inference without giving the analyst unseen knowledge. | `DYN-001-N`: every participant silently acquires information available only to the report's compiler. | `DYN-001-B`: incomplete records leave responsibility uncertain; the next required operational step is still explicit. |
| `RSR-DYN-002`: belief validity, counterevidence, recognition, or uptake matters | `DYN-002-P`: a reliable handover earns confidence in this procedure before justifying broader reliance. | `DYN-002-N`: confidence from an obsolete process transfers unchanged after its conditions change. | `DYN-002-B`: decisive evidence can warrant immediate revision; delay is not obligatory. |
| `RSR-DYN-003`: trust, assistance, authorization, or repair changes a relation | `DYN-003-P`: a participant stops an interview, requests a smaller topic, and resumes within that scope. | `DYN-003-N`: a useful result is treated as retroactive permission for an unauthorized intervention. | `DYN-003-B`: reliability about scheduling does not establish unrelated expertise or consent; urgency does not authorize unrelated control. |
| `RSR-DYN-004`: capability or protection relies on material supports or transferred costs | `DYN-004-P`: covering a colleague's shift improves one person's options while limiting the helper's next commitment. | `DYN-004-N`: a recorded equipment failure disappears when the plan next needs that equipment. | `DYN-004-B`: include only consequential dependencies; harmless help needs no invented hidden burden. |
| `RSR-DYN-005`: recurrence or selected artifact history contributes across the declared unit | `DYN-005-P`: a returned access card now permits a task previously unavailable to its holder. | `DYN-005-N`: a necessary recurring orientation cue is removed solely because it repeats a proposition. | `DYN-005-B`: steady recurrence can contribute rhythm or orientation without changing function; motifs remain optional. |
| `RSR-DYN-006`: intended communication and its reception can differ consequentially | `DYN-006-P`: a facilitator's joke offers brief shared relief while participants retain different practical concerns. | `DYN-006-N`: intended reassurance is reported as successful even after the recipient declines it. | `DYN-006-B`: humor and subtext are optional; an explicit explanation can be the necessary action. |
| `RSR-DYN-007`: completion affects participants or dimensions differently | `DYN-007-P`: an approved request improves access while a separate responsibility question remains unsettled. | `DYN-007-N`: procedural completion is presented as restored trust for every affected participant. | `DYN-007-B`: a genuinely complete resolution may end fully; no darker consequence or unfinished repair must be manufactured. |

## Combined review and replacement of weak shortcuts

- `DYN-C-001`: evidence plus uptake. Record a material observation, its source and validity, access, inference, uptake, and the next justified action. Truthfulness is separate from correctness; familiarity is separate from corroboration.
- `DYN-C-002`: trust plus authorization. A person may provide competent help, conceal another fact, and still honor a refusal. Assess those relations independently; affection and useful outcomes cannot certify permission.
- `DYN-C-003`: recurrence plus aftermath. Repeated acknowledgment can change recognition or feasible conduct even when the outcome is already known. Evaluate its contribution across the declared unit.
- `DYN-C-004`: support plus resolution. A successful intervention can redistribute capacity or cost. Preserve relevant downstream limits while permitting genuine relief.
- `DYN-C-005`: interpretive openness plus operative clarity. Preserve supported uncertainty in explanation while stating the immediate action, permission, consequence, and recovery explicitly wherever required.
- `DYN-C-006`: absent opportunity plus incomplete evidence. A document without an authorization change needs no invented negotiation; an applicable unreviewed change remains unknown.

Keep existing cadence, novelty, density, and word-threshold findings as bounded evidence. Replace their use as complete quality verdicts with contextual review under CTL-005/006/007/009/012/018. Repetition may change attention, recognition, confidence, reception, or deferred meaning without adding a new proposition. Layer counts do not establish depth. Sentence variance does not establish purposeful cadence. The first improvement does not always settle the appropriate exit.

The same review can support trimming explanatory echoes or interchangeable imagery when they add no independent contribution. Preserve orientation, processing, private information, and deferred dependencies. Direct explanation can be indispensable. These controls neither require restraint everywhere nor impose a concrete-to-abstract paragraph recipe.

## Review evidence and implementation boundary

An editorial record identifies the rule and revision, profile version and digest, target and candidate identities, audience/context, declared unit and span, semantic opportunity, finding, evidence, reviewer, correction, and recheck. Track materially relevant claims rather than every sentence. Scope trust and permission independently; supported partial improvement is neither universal repair nor universal failure.

Mechanical checks verify additive rule preservation, profile/build parity, closed declarative vocabulary, contextual runtime application, unknown manual-review findings, synthetic probe coverage, and rights-scope inventory. They cannot establish correct semantic interpretation, reader response, or overall quality. [Verification](relational-transitions-verification.md) records the bounded executed checks. No inherited rule, engine validator, request schema, protected priority, generation behavior, or historical license text is replaced.
